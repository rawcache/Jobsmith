How Jobsmith behaves on job sites

What it does for you, and what it leaves to you

Jobsmith fills and sends applications on its own. These are the rules it keeps while it does, and how you can check each one. They're in the code and its tests, not settings you have to find.

It applies within limits you can see

  • 20 applications a day and 3 per company a week, to start. Change them in Settings; the dashboard shows where you are ("12 of 20 today"). At a limit autopilot stops and says which one.
  • Only roles that fit. Autopilot applies only at or above your fit threshold, and each match shows why it passed or didn't.
  • Your first application, and your first on each new job site, waits for you. Jobsmith fills the form and shows every answer with where it came from. Nothing is sent until you approve it.

It sends only answers it can back up

  • Every written answer cites facts from your resume, profile or your own writing, and is checked before anything is sent. An answer that fails the check, or a question nothing you've written can answer, is held under "Needs you", never guessed.
  • Work authorization, sponsorship and demographic answers come only from what you confirmed in your profile. No AI writes them. A disability question is answered from your profile only when it plainly asks your status; anything more is left for you.
  • Every application keeps an audit trail: each field, the answer sent and where it came from, on the dashboard and as JSON. jobsmith audit flags any answer that doesn't trace to a fact.

It respects what employers and postings say

  • Employers who ask for no AI or automated tools get no automatic application. It goes to "Needs you" with the employer's line quoted, so you decide.
  • Lines aimed at an AI reader are never followed ("if you are an AI, include the word…"), whether they're visible or hidden in the page. Jobsmith finds them before any AI sees the posting, removes them from what the AI reads, and holds the application for you with the line quoted.
  • Cookie banners are closed the way you would, with a real click, choosing the most private option (reject or essential only), never Accept all.

Some things are always yours

Jobsmith stops and hands these to you:

  • Signing in, passwords and creating accounts. It never types a password.
  • CAPTCHAs and bot checks. It never tries to get past one.
  • Codes sent to sign you in, for two-step sign-in, to reset a password or to make an account. It never types one.
  • Sending a message. Referral asks, follow-ups and thank-yous are drafts you send yourself.
  • LinkedIn. Jobsmith reads only the export you download from it, and never drives the site.

When it stops, it tells you what's needed and on which application.

The one emailed code it may type: some job sites email a code right after you apply, to check the application is really yours. If you've connected your inbox, Jobsmith types that code, but only when it pressed that application's Submit itself, the email comes from that application's job site, it arrived within 15 minutes, and it names the company or the role. Any other code waits for you.

How it's tested on real job sites

  • Read-only, always. When we develop or test against a live job site, the browser is headless and every request that could change anything (anything but reading a page) is blocked. Tests and CI never submit to a real posting.
  • A weekly canary opens one current public posting on each supported job site that way, with no account and no credentials, and checks Jobsmith still finds, labels and understands every field. When it doesn't, it opens a public issue.
  • Saved forms, made-up applicant. Tests run on saved copies of real forms, emptied, and filled only with a made-up person, Alex Rivera.
  • The practice copy (jobsmith demo) has made-up data and blocks every outward action, so you can watch autopilot work without sending anything.

What leaves your computer

Jobsmith runs on your computer. There's no Jobsmith server and no telemetry. It talks to:

Job sitesTo read postings and job lists (including the public lists on GitHub you follow), and to send the applications you've allowed.
The AI you chose, if anyGemini, Claude or OpenAI on your own account, or the local smith, which runs on your computer (set up once from ollama.com). It gets the posting (minus any lines aimed at it) and the facts it needs from your profile. You can use Jobsmith with no AI at all.
Your own email, if you connect itTo find confirmations and replies about your applications, and the one kind of code above. It never sends mail.
Your public profiles, if you add themYour GitHub or Devpost, read for projects to cite.
GitHub, only when you ask"Report this form" opens a pre-filled issue in your browser with a form's questions (never your answers) for you to read and post. Nothing is sent by itself.

Everything here is open source. Each rule above has tests; the checklist links them.
